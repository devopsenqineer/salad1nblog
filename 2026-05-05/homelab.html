Homelabbing is not just Homelabbing.

What do I mean by that?

Most of us are IT people at heart. You probably deal with the exact same stuff at work every day and see where the industry is heading. It feels like IT today expects you to have a GitHub repo packed with projects, senior-level skills for a junior’s salary, and of course “more AI” needs to be crammed in everywhere.

So why the hell would you voluntarily spend your free time dealing with even more of it?

Simple: genuine interest. Real growth. The green field. Just testing things, being patient when stuff breaks, and diving into tech in your spare time to actually get better – completely stress-free. You can shut it off whenever you feel like it and pick up right where you left off.

Rome wasn’t built in a day either. ;)

Quick Real Talk

I’ve worked with these technologies professionally for a while and wanted a production-ready, disposable cluster deployment.

To test new applications on a clean slate.

I’m a DevOps engineer with experience across various cloud providers, on-prem setups and a solid amount of Terraform work under my belt.

GitOps wasn’t new to me either, so the only real shift was moving from enterprise K8s at work to K3s at home – same thing, just smaller.

So I’ve got a production-ready deployment with its own SDNs, its own firewall rules, its own VLANs, internet access only for downloads, but completely unreachable from outside.

Also planning to add another external base service for better monitoring and an extra security layer. Might introduce a dedicated firewall base service. Policies with Kyverno are also on the list.

Technology

Alright, I’ve kept it suspenseful long enough – here are the technologies.

Zone 1: Edge & DNS Layer

This is how the setup talks to the internet. The blue dotted line shows the public traffic. I have absolutely no interest in hanging my network unprotected on the open internet, so everything runs through Cloudflare (TLS/DNS). Cloudflare filters out the garbage and passes the clean traffic through to me.

Inside the cluster, MetalLB picks up the packet (because we’re on-prem and don’t have ready-made cloud load balancers) and hands it off to Traefik. Traefik is my ingress controller. Every new app I deploy (e.g. app.domain.dev) automatically gets a fresh certificate from Traefik via the Cloudflare DNS challenge.

One small detail: so I don’t have to take an unnecessary detour through the internet when I’m on my home Wi-Fi, AdGuard Home runs as a base service acting as split-DNS. When I’m home, AdGuard resolves the domain directly to the internal MetalLB IP. Zero-trust vibes at full LAN speed.

Zone 2: Control & Management (VLAN 20)

This is the brain. My MGMT node runs on Proxmox, backed by a hefty ZFS storage pool. Why the strict separation? Because this is where everything gets provisioned from.

At the SDN level, I’ve configured the VNets so the machines can pull stuff from the internet (updates, images etc.), but they’re completely invisible and unreachable from the outside.

The purple line shows the management access: from my VSCode server on the MGMT node, I push the infrastructure together via Terraform.

Zone 3: K3s Workload Cluster (VLAN 40)

This is the actual playground. A lightweight K3s cluster for my applications. And no manual kubectl messing around here. The whole setup runs on a GitOps approach.

I push my code to my local Gitea instance. Then ArgoCD comes into play: it monitors my repo, spots the new manifests and automatically pulls the cluster to the desired state.

A full enterprise CI/CD pipeline, running entirely locally.
